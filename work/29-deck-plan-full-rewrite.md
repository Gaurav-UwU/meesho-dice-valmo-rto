## Slide 1: Executive summary (ask ①) · NEW
**Headline:** Pay for the hard stop, check it was real, recover what still fails: 3 more of every 100 orders delivered
**Message:** Valmo pays the same for an easy and a hard stop, so hard stops fail. Round 2 research shows three kinds of failure; we give each its own fix, built on systems Valmo already has, and test them fairly before scaling.

**① The problem** (four big-number tiles)
- **17%** of Valmo orders fail (data pack) · **₹170 vs ₹50** per failed vs delivered order · **96%** of failures are COD · **2 in 3** fail at the door
- Mini bar: RTO by distance from the hub 15% / 17% / 22%

**② What we found** (➢ bullets)
- ➢ Two-sided friction: refusing COD costs the customer nothing; the rider is paid flat and only on success (Valmo's own contract).
- ➢ **Since Round 1 (field research in metro and Tier 3/4):** parcels arrive before the promised date and COD customers don't have cash ready; addresses are landmarks, not house numbers; hubs earn ₹5 per delivered parcel; riders want a per-order bonus.
- ➢ So failures come in **three kinds**: *not ready* (wants it, wrong moment), *not wanting* (changed mind), *not reached* (a faked attempt, a bad address, the wrong hub).
- Evidence line: 12 rider interviews · COD buyer survey (25+) · Round 2 field research **[n]** · real test orders · Valmo Delivery Services Agreement · RHP

**③ What we recommend** (stacked cards, Round 1 codes)
- **Now: R1 Rescue Bonus:** ₹15 when a top-20% risky order is delivered: **−3 RTO points, ₹62–184 cr a year**, if a 30-day paired-rider pilot confirms it.
- **Now, alongside: R2 fake-attempt control** (hub captain reviews suspicious attempts) **+ P1 early-arrival heads-up** ("pay by UPI, or keep my promised date").
- **Next: C1 Refused-Parcel Router:** ₹50–140 cr a year (up to ₹270 cr). **P2 learned doorsteps** in a small-town hub.
- **Long-term: pay by difficulty;** every delivery teaches the map.
- Under the cards: *"Even if the bonus fails, fake-attempt control and the Router stand on their own."*

**Callout band:** the brief's three tests, as a scorecard: delivery cost ↓ · rider earnings ↑ · customer speed and ease unchanged. Plus the prototype **QR** (Demo landing page) and *"built and tested in our prototype"*.
**Footer:** case data pack · Meesho RHP and Q1 FY27 letter · Valmo Delivery Services Agreement · our field research (Round 1 + Round 2).

## Slide 2: Where and why RTO happens (ask ②) · Round 1 slide 1, upgraded
**Headline:** Two-thirds of RTOs happen at a door a rider can still save, and many "refusals" just weren't ready
**Message:** COD is where failure lives; most of it happens at the door, for reasons on both sides, and Round 2 research shows a large part is timing, not intent.

**① Where: COD is the residual** (keep Round 1's charts and numbers)
- RTO 21.2% → 18.6% → 17.8% (FY23–25): a 16% fall, about three-quarters from prepaid. But COD success slipped 78.6% → 77.7%: **that lever is spent.**
- COD fails at 22.3% vs 2.7% prepaid (8×); **96%** of failures are COD while COD is 77% of shipments.
- India is not one market: **18%** in Vadodara vs **35%** in Patna; RTO 22% → 35% as delivery time goes from 1–2 to 5+ days.

**② Why: the Round 1 pie, regrouped into three kinds** (the pie on the left, three coloured blocks on the right)
| Kind | From the pie | Our evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Not ready** | not home 18 · unreachable 12 · the "no cash" part of refused 36 | Parcels arrive early with no warning (Tier 3/4 research; our order: promised 3 Oct, came 28 Sep) |
| **Not wanting** | the "cheaper elsewhere / changed mind" part of refused 36 | "We order on COD and keep watching prices on other apps" (Round 1 survey) |
| **Not reached** | unclear address 13 · far/wrong hub 9 · no real attempt 9 | "For COD we call once or twice… and we do not go back" (Round 1 riders); landmarks, not house numbers (Tier 3/4) |
- Refusal split chart: **real survey numbers here** (the Round 1 survey asked "why did you refuse?" with "I did not have cash at that moment" as an option: count the sheet). Until then, label it *"Illustrative; the pilot measures it"* (see the placeholder rules in 28).

**③ Two-sided friction** (loop diagram + the real-order strip)
- Customer: nothing paid, so refusing is free; intent decays in transit.
- Rider: paid only on success, flat per stop, so effort goes to likely successes; the cheapest exit from a hard stop is a weak or fake attempt.
- Loop: expected to fail → less effort → fails → expectation confirmed.
- Real-order strip (redrawn, no phone numbers): COD order → arrived 5 days early, no heads-up → one-way "Arriving Today" → **the rider called twice and didn't wait** → "Failed Delivery".

**Methods strip (footer, research-quality points):** 12 rider interviews (Mumbai hubs) · COD buyer survey, 25+ · Round 2 field research, metro + Tier 3/4 **[n]** · real test orders on Meesho + Flipkart · Valmo Delivery Services Agreement · RHP, Q4 FY26 and Q1 FY27 letters · case data pack · Shipway. Italic: *"What we don't know: whether riders cause COD failure or correctly predict it. The pilot's Control group measures it."*

## Slide 3: Prioritised solutions with expected impact (ask ③) · Round 1 slide 2, upgraded
**Headline:** Five new levers, scored: rescue at the door first, because it needs no new system
**Message:** Same framework as Round 1 (Prevent · Rescue · Recover, impact × effort), now with numbers, and each lever matched to a kind of failure.

**① The Round 1 map, updated** (impact × effort; arrows show what moved since Round 1)
- R1 stays in **Pursue**.
- **R2 (fake-attempt control) moves Consider → Pursue:** Valmo already collects the evidence (verification call, GPS, calls), and the hub, paid only on delivery, wants real attempts.
- **P1's cheapest slice moves to Pursue:** an early-arrival heads-up needs no build.
- P2 (learned doorsteps) and C1 stay in Assess: they need a hub trial / legal controls first.

**② Scored table** (the main element)
| Lever | Kind of failure it fixes | Assumed effect | RTO pts | ₹ cr / yr | Effort | When |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **R1 Rescue Bonus** | Not ready (rider effort), riskiest 20% | +15 deliveries per 100 flagged | −3.0 | 62 (conservative) – 184 (case) net | Low: Valmo's payout line exists | Now |
| **R2 Fake-attempt control** | Not reached: no real attempt 9% | measured in the baseline; a ₹10 review pays above ~1 in 10 recoveries | up to −1.5 (not claimed) | not claimed | Low | Now, all hubs, with or without the bonus |
| **P1 Heads-up + two-way WhatsApp** | Not ready: not home 18 + unreachable 12 | fixes ⅕ | −1.0 | ~95 gross | Low: Valmo already sends WhatsApp | Now (heads-up), day 31+ (two-way) |
| **P2 Learned doorsteps** | Not reached: address 13 + wrong hub 9 | fixes ¼ | −0.9 | ~80 gross | Med: builds on GeoIndia | Day 31+ in one hub |
| **C1 Refused-Parcel Router** | Not wanting: refused parcels | 2.5–13.5% re-homed + cheaper returns | 0 (recovers cost) | 50–270 | Med | Day 30+ |

**③ Already in play · excluded by design** (Round 1 list, kept)
- In play: prepaid nudges, TrustMesh filtering, predictive routing, reverse-network optimisation, address geocoding (GeoIndia), flexible delivery options, better customer communication.
- Excluded: COD restriction, convenience fees, forced prepaid, extra checkout steps: the brief rules out friction in ordering.
**Callout:** *"1 RTO point on Valmo ≈ 7.6 mn parcels ≈ ₹92 cr a year. Effects are planning assumptions the pilot replaces; P1, P2 and R2 overlap the bonus pool, so don't add them up."*

## Slide 9: The prototype (feasibility, presentation) · NEW
**Headline:** Built and tested: everything on these slides runs in our prototype
**Message:** A working console, not a mock-up: the bonus, the fake-attempt check, the refused-parcel router and the pilot verdict all run on the same rules the deck describes.

**① What you can do in six minutes** (a strip of four screenshots, left to right)
- **Rider app:** the "₹ +15 Bonus Eligible" chip, never the score; the strike meter (EN + Hindi).
- **Hub captain:** a disputed attempt with its evidence; confirm, free re-attempt or strike with a reason.
- **Refused-Parcel Desk:** inspection, the match forecast against the 5.5% break-even, the four second-chance options.
- **Pilot verdict:** GO / RE-PRICE / KILL with the range and the ✔ Fair comparison line.

**② What is real and what is simulated** (two-column table, from the landing page)
| Real code | Simulated |
|---|---|
| The pilot decision rule, pairing, safety rules, rule lock | The outcomes (the pilot hasn't run) |
| OTP rules, the ledger (pending → released / clawed back), the strike log and ladder | Orders, riders, names (synthetic, no customer data) |
| The Router's lanes and legal gates, booked savings only on real outcomes | Which parcels are refused; seller opt-ins |
| Live mode: phones synced through a realtime database, WhatsApp built | Real WhatsApp sending (blocked by Twilio's trial; Demo mode shows it on screen) |

**③ How we know it works**
- **1,253 automated tests**; **18 audit checks** run on every simulated day (nothing delivered without an OTP, nothing held without an inspection, every strike has a reason, the ledger matches every screen).
- Live-mode security: rider and captain keys split, rate limits, no customer data on screen.
**Callout:** the **QR code** (Demo landing page) + https://valmo-rescue-console.vercel.app · *"Try it: about six minutes, no sign-in."*
