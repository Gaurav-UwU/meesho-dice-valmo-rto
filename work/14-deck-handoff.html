The story in 60 seconds

Hypothesis (from Round 1)

COD fails because of friction on both sides of the door.

  • Customer side: nothing is paid upfront, so refusing, changing your mind or not being home costs nothing.
  • Rider side: riders are paid only on success, and the same for easy and hard stops. So risky COD stops get less effort: two calls, no wait, move on.

Through-line for Round 2

Meesho already predicts which orders will fail (its TrustMesh model), but that signal stops before dispatch. The rider at the door never sees it. We carry that signal to the door and beyond, and trigger the cheapest fix at each stage.

Ask ④ Refused / undelivered orders

Send each refused parcel to its cheapest legal recovery. Only the rest travels back

Message: A per-parcel router with three lanes, each checked against GST, consumer, privacy and foreign-investment rules.
Layout: 3 panels + callout
1Why it matters
  • A return costs ₹120 = 45% of Meesho's ₹265 average order
  • Meesho bears it (sellers aren't charged for RTO)
  • ~130 mn RTO parcels a year (763.5 mn × 17%)
  • Industry default: send it back (Shopee: refuse once → return to sender)
  • No marketplace re-homes refused parcels from the last-mile hub. Prior art is patents (Pitney Bowes, expired; Shopify) + one small Indian firm doing it from its own warehouse
2The Router (3 lanes)
  1. Second chance clean: WhatsApp reschedule · hub pickup · other address in the same state · pay now. Saves the original sale
  2. Hold & Re-home clean if same state: unopened, seal checked, seller state = hub state = buyer state, seller opted in for that product. Saves ~₹145 per match
  3. Batched return clean: everything else, grouped by seller. US programmes report up to 20–40% cheaper
Decision diagram with a legal tick on each lane
3Hold & Re-home: break-even + rules
  • Hold 48h ≈ ₹8 · match saves ₹145 → break-even 5.5% match rate (we don't claim a match rate)
  • Sizing: ~₹50 / 140 / 270 cr at 2.5 / 7 / 13.5% effective match
  • Start with non-GST sellers: by law they sell only within their own state, so every refused parcel already qualifies
  • Pilot in Uttar Pradesh (#1 seller state, 15.9% of sellers)
  • Seller opts in and sets rules; the first nearby order gets it; the seller invoices; ownership never passes to Meesho
  • Router screenshot PENDING
Break-even line chart
The pilot measures first: how long refused parcels sit at the hub today, the share of "maybe later" refusals, and the match rate. Kill if the match rate is below 3% after 30 days or any custody incident happens.
Footer: GST s.2(85), IGST s.10(1)(a), Notif. 34/2023-CT · FDI Press Note 2 (2018) · Consumer Protection (E-Com) Rules 2020 · DPDP Act 2023 · UPS Happy Returns / Optoro · Meesho RHP (seller states).

10x and long-term thinking

Today the risk score is a filter. Tomorrow it's a price

Layout: 3 panels + one-line callout
1Three horizons
  • Now: one ₹15 bonus on the riskiest 20%
  • 6–12 months: a price per parcel by difficulty (risk × distance × address × time slot)
  • Long-term: every node paid per successful outcome; refused parcels become a local inventory network
Staircase graphic
2Judge carriers by cost per success
  • Meesho already gives each lane to its cheapest provider (Q1 FY27 call)
  • Cost per success = (forward + RTO% × ₹120) ÷ (1 − RTO%)
  • Valmo: ₹84.8 today → ₹77.7 at 14% RTO
  • Carrier A ₹45 @10% RTO = ₹63; carrier B ₹40 @20% = ₹80. The cheaper parcel is the dearer delivery
Bar chart A vs B, per parcel vs per success
3It learns, and stays asset-light
  • Pilot data → learn which orders effort saves → set prices
  • Works for Valmo and 3PL partners alike
  • Software + incentives on existing floor space; no warehouses (Vidit Aatrey: warehousing "tends to have lower ROI")
One line to remember: "Money that carries no information can't coordinate a network. Price the difficulty."
