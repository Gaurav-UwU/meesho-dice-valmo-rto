# Why each question is in the script

Read this **before** going out, not during. Once you know why each question exists you can improvise when a rider takes the conversation somewhere better — which they will.

Companion to [03-interview-script.md](03-interview-script.md).

---

## WARM-UP — three questions that look like small talk

| Question | Why it's there |
|---|---|
| How long have you been doing this? Which hub? | Calibrates weight — a 3-month and a 3-year rider tell you different things. **Hub matters more:** if all 10 riders are from one hub, you've measured one hub, not Valmo. Know that before generalising on a slide. |
| How many deliveries a day? | Your denominator. No rate without it. Sanity-checks everything said later. |
| How are you paid — per delivery or fixed? | Foundational fact for spike A. Asked **early and neutrally**, before they know you care about pay — ask it later and the answer gets shaped by what they think you want. The case *asserts* flat per-success pay; verify it, and whether it varies by hub/partner. |

---

## SIZING BLOCK

| Question | Why it's there |
|---|---|
| How many parcels yesterday? Delivered? Returned? | Anchored to a **specific day** on purpose — memory for yesterday is reliable, memory for "usually" is invention. Across 10 riders this gives your **own** RTO rate to set against the pack's 17% and the prospectus-derived 17.8%. Matching the audited number is a credibility moment; diverging from it is a finding. |
| Reason for each — **one by one** | Highest-value question in the script. "One by one" is doing the work: ask for a summary and you get an impression ("mostly not home"); ask for enumeration and you get **countable data**. This produces a cause-split that does not exist publicly for India. |
| Was yesterday normal? | Sample calibration. If most say it was unusual, your sizing is skewed and you must say so. Stated limitations score; unstated ones get found. |

## NARRATIVE — the last failure, whole story

Buckets flatten causal chains. A parcel logged "customer not home" might actually be: *attempted 2pm on a weekday, no call made, marked failed.* The bucket says demand problem; the story says scheduling problem. **This question is how you discover your buckets are wrong.**

## DISCRIMINATOR A — rider incentives

| Question | Why it's there |
|---|---|
| Which parcels do you leave for last? | Non-accusatory entry. "Leaving for last" is socially acceptable to admit; "skipping" isn't. The categories they name are the **risk features for a model**. |
| Which delivery annoys you most? | Emotional framing surfaces what rational framing misses. Also warms them up before the sensitive one. |
| Far parcel, phone not connecting, same money as a nearby one — what do you do? | The core test, put as a **situation** not an accusation. Deliberately does not ask "is that unfair?" — that invites complaint, not information. |
| *Some riders say they mark 'not reachable' without going. Does that happen?* | Most sensitive, highest value. The case names fake attempts; nobody has quantified them. **Third-person projection**: people describe their own conduct readily when it's attributed to a category. Asked directly → universal denial. **The follow-up percentage matters far more than the yes.** |
| Would more money help? → **How much more?** | The first half is worthless — everyone says yes to more money. The second half gives you a **price**. If ₹20 makes a hard delivery worth doing, set that against the ₹120 reverse cost and show the arithmetic closes. This is what turns the spike from a wish into a costed proposal. |

## DISCRIMINATOR B — local resale

| Question | Why it's there |
|---|---|
| What happens to a refused parcel? Where does it go? | Documents a physical process no public source describes. Needed to know where an intervention could sit. |
| **How many days does it sit at the hub?** | **GO/NO-GO for this spike.** Dwell time *is* the resale window. Same-day dispatch → no window → idea is operationally dead. Three days → real. |
| Has anyone ever asked to buy a returned parcel? | Tests latent demand **without proposing the idea** — measuring, not leading. Also surfaces the grey-market reality, which you must answer anyway. |
| Do people in the same building order similar things? | Resale needs a nearby buyer for *that specific item*. Uncorrelated local demand → matching fails → doesn't scale. |

---

## DISCRIMINATOR C — availability

| Question | Why it's there |
|---|---|
| What time do deliveries fail most? | Clustering → timing is causal. Uniform → it isn't. |
| Do you decide when to go, or does the app? | The spike needs timing to be controllable **and** currently mis-set. The peer-reviewed work (Kandula et al. 2021) says routes optimise for distance, not customer availability — check whether that holds here. |
| Would a different time have worked? | Tests **recoverability**. If yes, "not at home" is a scheduling failure, not a demand failure. Entirely different problem, entirely different fix. |
| Do you know which customer will be around when? | Sharpest question in the block. If experienced riders already know tacitly, the problem isn't prediction — it's that **nobody captures what riders already know**. Far easier prototype, far better pitch than "predict from scratch." |

## CLOSE

| Question | Why it's there |
|---|---|
| If you could change one thing? | Unprimed and open. Best source of whatever your framework missed. |
| Biggest reason deliveries fail, in your view? | Deliberately set **against their own tally** from the sizing block. When the count says "not at home" dominates but they *say* "customers change their mind," that gap between **frequent and salient** is genuinely interesting — and reads as real research. |

## HUB OPERATORS — same logic, different altitude

Riders give you one route's experience. Operators give you hundreds of orders and the **only** view of wrong-hub routing and attempt integrity.

The question to listen hardest on:
> *"If you were allowed to sell that parcel to someone nearby instead of sending it back — could you? What would stop you?"*

Their objection **is your risks slide**, written by someone who actually runs the node. Don't argue with it. Write it down verbatim.

---

## THE META-RULE

Every question above exists to produce one of three things:

1. **A number** you can put on Slide 1
2. **A discriminator** that decides the spike tonight
3. **A quote** that proves you were actually there

If a question in the field isn't producing one of those, drop it and move on. You have eight minutes and they're being paid per delivery.
