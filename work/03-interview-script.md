# Field script — Wednesday, Sep 9

Built to do three jobs at once:
1. **Produce a cause-split for Slide 1** (with numbers, from primary data)
2. **Decide the spike** — questions are designed to discriminate between the three candidates
3. **Generate quotable evidence** for the "Extent of Research" criterion

Tag key on every question: `[SIZE]` = feeds Slide 1 sizing · `[A]` = tests rider-incentive spike · `[B]` = tests local-resale spike · `[C]` = tests availability-prediction spike · `[Q]` = quote-hunting

---

## GROUND RULES — read before going out

**Riders are paid per successful delivery. Your 8 minutes costs them money.**
- Approach riders who are **waiting, sorting, or on a break** — never mid-delivery.
- Offer chai or ₹50–100 up front, before asking anything. Say it's for their time. Don't make it conditional on answering.
- Cap at **8 minutes**. Set a timer. If they're engaged and willing, keep going — but you offered 8.
- Open with what you're doing: *"College project on why deliveries fail. Not from any company, nothing goes to your manager."* This matters — riders will not tell you about attempt-marking if they think you're an auditor.
- **Ask permission to take notes.** Don't record audio unless they say yes.
- Write down numbers **on the spot**. You will not remember rider 6's figures by rider 9.

**Interviewing rules that decide whether this data is worth anything:**
- Ask about **the last specific instance**, never the general case. "Tell me about the last delivery that came back" beats "why do deliveries fail."
- **Never** ask "would you like it if…" — hypotheticals produce agreement, not information.
- Let silence sit. The second thing they say is usually the real answer.
- If they say something surprising, **stop and dig**. That's the deck.

**Target: 8–10 riders, 1–2 hub operators.** More riders beats more depth per rider.

## PART 1 — RIDER SCRIPT (~8 min)

### Warm-up (60 sec)
> "Kitne time se ye kaam kar rahe ho? Kaunse hub se ho?"
> *How long have you been doing this? Which hub are you with?*

> "Ek din mein kitni delivery milti hai aapko?"
> *How many deliveries do you get in a day?*

> "Payment kaise hota hai — per delivery, ya fixed?"
> *How are you paid — per delivery, or fixed?* `[A]`

### 🔥 THE SIZING BLOCK — the most valuable questions in this script `[SIZE]`

There is **no credible published breakdown of RTO causes in India.** If ten riders answer this, you own a number nobody else has. Get it precisely.

> "Kal kitne parcel mile the aapko?"
> *How many parcels did you get yesterday?* → `____`

> "Usme se kitne deliver ho gaye?"
> *How many got delivered?* → `____`

> "Aur kitne wapas gaye?"
> *And how many went back?* → `____`

> "Jo wapas gaye — unme kya kya reason tha? Ek ek karke batao."
> *The ones that went back — what was the reason for each? Go one by one.*

**Tally each returned parcel into a bucket. Do not summarise for them — make them enumerate:**

| Bucket | Count |
|---|---|
| Customer not at home | |
| Customer refused at the door | |
| Phone not reachable / switched off | |
| Address wrong or couldn't find it | |
| Customer said they didn't order it | |
| Didn't have money (COD) | |
| Other → write verbatim | |

> "Ye normal din tha ya zyada/kam tha?"
> *Was yesterday normal, or higher/lower than usual?* — calibrates the sample.

---

### Narrative — the last failure `[Q]`
> "Sabse recent parcel jo wapas gaya — pura bataiye kya hua tha."
> *The most recent parcel that went back — tell me the whole story.*

Let them talk. Don't interrupt. Then probe: what time was it, how many times did you go, did you call, what did they say?

---

### Discriminator A — rider intent and pay `[A]`

Sensitive. Use third-person projection so they're describing "other riders," not confessing.

> "Kuch parcels aise hote hain jo aap last mein rakhte ho? Kaunse?"
> *Are there parcels you leave for last? Which ones?*

> "Kaunsi delivery sabse zyada pareshan karti hai?"
> *Which kind of delivery is the most annoying?*

> "Agar ek parcel bahut door hai, ya customer ka phone nahi lag raha — aur aapko ussi ka utna hi paisa milta hai jitna paas wale ka. Tab kya karte ho?"
> *If a parcel is very far, or the customer's phone isn't connecting — and you get the same money as a nearby one. What do you do then?*

> "Kuch riders bolte hain ki jab trip worth nahi hoti, toh 'customer not reachable' mark kar dete hain bina gaye. Aisa hota hai?"
> *Some riders say that when a trip isn't worth it, they mark 'customer not reachable' without going. Does that happen?*
> → **If yes: "kitne percent orders mein aisa hota hoga?"** *(what percent of orders?)*

> "Mushkil delivery ke liye zyada paisa milta toh karte?"
> *If you got more money for a difficult delivery, would you do it?* → then immediately: **"kitna zyada?"** *(how much more?)* — the number matters, the yes doesn't.

---

### Discriminator B — what happens to a refused parcel `[B]`

> "Jab customer mana kar deta hai — parcel ka kya karte ho? Kahan jaata hai?"
> *When a customer refuses — what do you do with the parcel? Where does it go?*

> "Hub pe kitne din pada rehta hai wapas jaane se pehle?"
> *How many days does it sit at the hub before going back?*

> "Kabhi kisi ne poocha ki ye parcel mujhe bech do?"
> *Has anyone ever asked you to just sell them a parcel?* → **critical. Establishes latent local demand.**

> "Same building ya area mein log same type ka saman order karte hain?"
> *Do people in the same building or area order similar things?*

---

### Discriminator C — timing and availability `[C]`

> "Kis time pe delivery sabse zyada fail hoti hai?"
> *What time of day do deliveries fail most?*

> "Aap decide karte ho kab jaana hai, ya app batata hai?"
> *Do you decide when to go, or does the app tell you?*

> "Jo customer ghar pe nahi mila — agar aap doosre time pe jaate, toh mil jaata?"
> *The customer who wasn't home — if you'd gone at a different time, would you have found them?*

> "Aapko pata hota hai ki kaunsa customer kis time milega?"
> *Do you know which customer will be available at what time?*

---

### Close `[Q]`
> "Ek cheez badal sakte ho is kaam mein — kya badloge?"
> *If you could change one thing about this job — what would it be?*

> "Aapke hisaab se delivery fail kyun hoti hai, sabse badi wajah?"
> *In your view, what's the single biggest reason deliveries fail?*

## PART 2 — HUB OPERATOR SCRIPT (~15 min)

Harder to get, worth far more. They see hundreds of orders. If you land one, this is your best slide-2 material.

**Volume & baseline** `[SIZE]`
- How many parcels through this hub per day? How many riders?
- Of 100 parcels, how many come back? Has that changed over the last year?
- Which areas or pin codes are worst? Why those?

**Wrong-hub routing**
- Do parcels arrive here that should have gone elsewhere? How often? What do you do with them?
- How much time does that add?

**Attempt integrity** `[A]`
- How do you know a rider actually attempted a delivery?
- Can you tell when someone marks an attempt without going? What tips you off?
- What happens to a rider who does that?

**Rider economics** `[A]`
- How are riders paid here? Same rate for every delivery?
- Do riders push back on certain orders? Which ones?
- How do you allocate the difficult ones?

**Reverse flow — the resale test** `[B]`
- Walk me through what physically happens to a refused parcel from the moment it comes back to you.
- How long does it sit here? Where? Who pays for that space?
- How many go back to the seller each week?
- *"If you were allowed to sell that parcel to someone nearby instead of sending it back — could you? What would stop you?"* → **listen hard for the objection; that's your risks slide**

**Timing** `[C]`
- What time do you dispatch? Do riders re-attempt at a different time of day?
- If you could pick the delivery time per customer, would it help?

## PART 3 — COD SHOPPER SURVEY (Google Form)

Build this **before you go out** so it collects while you're in the field. Target ~100 responses, metro + hometown.

**Screener**
1. Have you ordered online with Cash on Delivery in the last 6 months? *(Yes/No — No exits)*
2. Which city/town do you order to? *(free text — gives you the metro vs T2/T3 cut)*
3. Which platforms? *(multi: Meesho / Amazon / Flipkart / Myntra / other)*

**Refusal behaviour — the core** `[SIZE]`
4. In the last 6 months, roughly how many COD orders did you refuse or not accept at the door? *(0 / 1 / 2–3 / 4+)*
5. If you refused any — why? *(multi: changed my mind · found it cheaper elsewhere · took too long to arrive · wasn't home and never rescheduled · didn't have cash · ordered by mistake · someone else in family had ordered it · looked different from photos · other)*
6. Have you ever ordered something knowing you might not accept it? *(Yes/No/Maybe)* — **this one gets at intent-to-refuse, which nobody measures**

**Availability** `[C]`
7. What time are you usually available to receive a delivery? *(morning / afternoon / evening / late evening / varies)*
8. Has a delivery failed because you weren't home? *(Yes/No)*
9. Would you use a feature to choose your delivery time slot? *(Yes/No/Maybe)*

**Address & contact**
10. Does your address have a proper house/street number, or is it landmark-based? *(proper / landmark-based / mix)*
11. Have you missed a delivery because the rider couldn't reach you by phone? *(Yes/No)*

**Payment** `[SIZE]`
12. Why do you choose COD over prepaid? *(multi: don't trust the platform · want to check before paying · no UPI/card · habit · returns feel easier · other)*
13. What would make you pay in advance? *(multi: discount · guaranteed easy return · a brand I trust · nothing · other)*

**Open** `[Q]`
14. Tell us about the last time you refused or missed a delivery. *(free text — this is where the quotes come from)*

## PART 4 — PRE-COMMITTED DECISION RULE

**Write your call down tonight against these thresholds, before you start rationalising.** Deciding the rule *before* seeing the data is what stops you picking the spike you already liked.

**Spike A — rider incentives** wins if:
- ≥5 of 10 riders describe deprioritising or skipping specific order types, **and** name pay/distance as the reason
- **or** any rider confirms attempt-marking without attempting, with a rough frequency

**Spike B — local resale** wins if:
- Refused parcels sit at the hub ≥2 days before going back, **and**
- ≥3 riders/operators report someone having asked to buy a returned parcel, or clear local demand for the same goods

**Spike C — availability prediction** wins if:
- "Not at home" is the largest single bucket in the sizing tally, **and**
- riders say a different attempt time would have worked, **and** they don't control attempt timing

**If two tie → pick A.** It's the documented white space, it fits a 30-day pilot, and it's the one your fieldwork will have the most original evidence for.
**If nothing clears its bar** → the honest answer is that "not at home" plus payment mode dominate, and the spike becomes C with A as the mechanism.
