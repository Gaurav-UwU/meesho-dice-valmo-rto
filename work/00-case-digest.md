# DICE Challenge 3.0 — All 6 Problem Statements (digest)

Source: 6 PDFs in `Meesho/`. Read 2026-09-08. This file replaces the PDFs for working purposes.

## Shared structure (identical across all 6)

**Round 1 — 3 slides.** Evaluated on: (1) Extent of Research — methods, sources, coverage; (2) Quality of Insights; (3) User-First Approach; (4) Problem-First Mindset.

**Round 2 — 6–10 slides + working prototype.** Evaluated on: (1) Quality of Research; (2) Depth of Analysis; (3) Innovativeness; (4) **10x and Long-term Thinking**; (5) Feasibility; (6) Presentation Skills.

Note: Round-1 scoring has *zero* explicit weight on solution quality. Three of four criteria are about **research and problem framing**. The solution slide is scored as "problem-first mindset," not as a good idea.

Several cases explicitly instruct: **speak to 7–10 real people** + float a survey. This is a primary-research competition wearing a case-comp costume.

## 1. UG — Winning Male Users on Meesho

**Objective:** Increase penetration + LTV among male users — new acquisition, category expansion, retention — without diluting the female-first brand equity. Horizon 3–5 years.

**Stated barriers:**
- Male users "sticky" to competition — electronics/mobile accessories, where rivals have brand assurance, financing, after-sales credibility
- Perception "Meesho is for women" — reinforced by catalog, merchandising, influencer choices
- Quality/returns concerns felt more acutely by male users
- Category depth missing: electronics, gadgets, sports, auto accessories
- Even when acquired: weak activation, poor repeat, LTV far below platform average

**R1 asks:** (a) what does a first-time male user expect from a marketplace; which male sub-segment/category first? (b) what business-model changes (categories, QA, fulfillment, returns policy, brand/marketing) are needed? (c) media plan (story + distribution), sizing, GTM.

**R2 adds:** exec summary, avenues for acquisition + LTV, TAM/gap/trigger/competitor analysis, resource enhancement, GTM incl. media plan, short + long-term recs, KPIs, risks (incl. diluting female-first equity), top-line and bottom-line plan.

**Research access:** easy. Male non-users are everywhere. You are one.

## 2. Monetization — ~1% of NMV in genuinely new annual revenue

**Objective:** New revenue stream worth ~1% of NMV, without breaking affordability for buyers or zero-commission for sellers.

**Existing levers (explicitly off-limits as "the answer"):** advertising, logistics fees, seller tools, assurance programs, financial services. "More ads" / "higher delivery fees" is called out as the lazy answer.

**Explicitly widened scope:** not restricted to current Meesho users. Can serve kirana stores, B2B SMEs, wholesalers, manufacturers. Names IndiaMART-style and B2B e-commerce as example directions.

**Challenges:** avoid repeating existing levers; prove willingness to pay; must serve *small* sellers not just large brands; protect affordability.

**R1 asks:** The Opportunity (real unmet need + why it becomes revenue) / The Idea (what's offered, how it's paid for) / Why It Fits (genuinely new + consistent with affordability + zero commission).

**R2 adds:** problem grounded in real conversations, solution + earn mechanism, **proof people will pay (evidence from conversations — would they, how much, what concerns)**, rough sizing with assumptions shown, 30-60-90 pilot, risks/guardrails.

**Explicit scoring guidance:** speak to 7–10 people; survey; secondary research on creative monetization globally; simple sizing with stated assumptions.

**Research access:** medium-hard. Needs seller/SME conversations. Kirana stores + local wholesalers are reachable.

## 3. Pricing — How should a new-to-online seller set and evolve price?

**Objective:** "A seller who has only ever sold offline is now listing on Meesho. How should they decide what price to set, and how should that decision evolve across the entire life of the product? And since not every seller has the understanding, data, or resources to do this well, how can Meesho help them?"

**The five ways online pricing differs from offline (given in the case):** price-sensitivity of demand (side-by-side comparison), true cost of doing business (packaging, payment handling, GST), post-sale losses (returns, RTO, damage, fraud/wardrobing/false "not received"), loss of ability to negotiate (list price *is* the whole negotiation), spends to drive visibility (ads, affiliate, discounts).

**Challenges:** low pricing literacy; no history for a brand-new product (cold start); moving target (demand/competition/cost drift); thin margins, low error tolerance; scale and diversity of seller base.

**R1 asks:** (a) how a seller reasons from factors to an actual number; (b) how that decision evolves over product life — what signals to watch, what triggers a revision; (c) core idea of how Meesho helps sellers who can't do this alone.

**R2 adds:** full factor set incl. the cold-start first price; evolution + trigger signals; the Meesho-side solution (what it is, how a seller uses it, reasoning); success measurement; key risks.

**Note:** this is the most *buildable* case — the deliverable is essentially a pricing engine/model. A working prototype maps 1:1 onto the ask.

**Research access:** hard-ish. Needs real sellers. But the analytical core can be built from unit economics without them.

## 4. SG (Seller Growth) — Grow C2M (manufacturer-direct) seller base

**Objective:** Grow Meesho's C2M seller base and their long-term contribution to price competitiveness.

**Framed as two connected problems:**
1. Large manufacturers don't come onto B2C at all — built for bulk, made-to-order, no returns; B2C means new ops, inventory risk, real cost of failure
2. Those who onboard don't stick — weak early volumes, conviction erodes, they exit before the channel matters

Explicitly: solving only one side does not move the needle.

**Challenges:** operational hassle (single-order processing, packing, reverse logistics — capabilities they lack); inventory risk (pre-stocking vs demand-confirmed B2B); unproven demand ("will there be enough scale?"); weak early scale-up (new sellers capture few orders in first 30 days).

**R1 asks:** Segment & Barrier Map (which manufacturer segments/categories to prioritise + top barriers) / Prioritisation Framework / Quick-Win Lever for first 30 days.

**R2 adds:** manufacturer segmentation by cohort (offline / online-active-elsewhere / previously churned), market sizing, onboarding model (reduce entry barriers — support stack, risk-sharing, assisted ops), sustainable scale-up model that works for hundreds of sellers without subsidy or manpower, metrics, 30-60-90, risks.

**Key nuance in the case:** scale is used today as the signal for who has a price advantage, but "scale alone does not always translate into a lower price on the platform." That's an invitation to build a better segmentation signal.

**Research access:** hardest. Requires access to actual manufacturers. Tirupur, Surat, Ludhiana, Panipat, Agra, Moradabad clusters.

## 5. CC (Content Commerce) — Influencer-led Beauty & Personal Care

**Objective:** Three-sided marketplace (brands / creators / end users). Design a model that aligns all three to: grow BPC NMV, increase creator retention in BPC, make influencer-led BPC commerce scalable.

**Context given:** BPC is high-consideration, trust-sensitive — shade matching, skin/hair suitability, ingredient awareness, counterfeit fear, especially for first-time online buyers in T2/T3. BPC is a meaningful share of Meesho NMV but a *smaller* share of affiliate NMV — under-indexed in the influencer channel. **Competitors run a higher affiliate share than Meesho in BPC.** Creators split into "Activated" (posting, no consistent traction) and "Power" (consistent NMV).

**Mechanics given:** creators order products from Meesho, produce content (tutorials, GRWM, hauls, reviews), share with a purchase link (Instagram autoDMs, captions, storefronts); users click through to Meesho to order.

**R1 asks:** Value Chain Map (map a BPC sale end-to-end when a creator drives it — *by talking with Meesho creators*) / Prioritisation Framework / Quick-Win Lever for first 30 days.

**R2 adds:** stage-by-stage value chain analysis with pain points, prioritised levers each with data-backed NMV sizing, deep dive on one lever, 30-60-90, supporting research from creator interviews / user surveys / expert conversations.

Also must answer: activation/sampling/incentive design for beauty creators; how trust & suitability concerns (shade, skin type, counterfeits) get resolved; what tech and **attribution** support is required; expected uplift + timeline.

**Research access:** easiest of all. Meesho creators are publicly discoverable on Instagram/YouTube and reachable by DM. Micro-creators reply.

## 6. Valmo — Reduce RTO (Return to Origin)

**Objective:** Identify biggest reasons behind failed deliveries on Valmo and design a strategy to bring RTO down without hurting delivery cost, rider earnings, or customer speed/ease.

**Network:** FM pickup hub → FM carting → source sort centre → national line haul → destination sort centre → regional line haul → last-mile DC → last-mile delivery. Local hubs run by small entrepreneurs; riders are gig workers paid per successful delivery.

**Given causes:**
- Fake/false "delivery attempted" markings by last-mile rider/partner
- Rider–customer unreachability by phone
- Unstructured addresses (landmark-based, no street names) → misrouting *and* hard for a new rider to locate
- Orders landing at the wrong delivery hub
- COD makes doorstep refusal frictionless
- Refused orders travel all the way back to seller at double logistics cost — **case explicitly asks: is there a cheaper way to handle reverse?**
- **Rider intent:** flat pay per successful delivery regardless of difficulty → riders under-serve hard orders
- India is not one market — dense-city solution ≠ village solution

**DATA PACK (the only case with hard numbers):**

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| COD share of Valmo orders | 80% |
| RTO% of COD orders | 20% |
| RTO% of prepaid orders | 5% |
| Forward logistics cost, successful delivery | ₹50/order |
| Reverse logistics cost, RTO shipment | ₹120/order |

Cost split per leg (₹): FM Hub 4 · FM Carting 2 · FMSC 5 · NLH 8 · LMSC 5 · RLH 5 · LMDC 21.
(Sums to 50. LMDC = 42% of forward cost — last mile dominates.)

Blended RTO = 0.8×20% + 0.2×5% = **17%**. Cost of an RTO = ₹50 forward (sunk) + ₹120 reverse = ₹170, vs ₹50 for a good delivery.

**R1 asks:** map biggest RTO reasons **with rough sizing of each** / prioritisation framework / one quick-win for first 30 days.

**R2 adds:** exec summary, where + why RTO happens backed by research and/or the data pack, prioritised solutions with expected impact, **a specific proposal for what to do with refused/undelivered orders instead of sending them back**, 30-60-90 with success measurement, risks.

**Explicit scoring guidance:** talk to delivery riders, hub operators, COD shoppers (any platform); place real Meesho orders and observe; survey on why customers refuse; study how other logistics players solved it; show sizing working; think about **second-order effects — does the idea hold once riders, hubs, or customers try to work around it?**

**Research access:** very easy. Riders, hub operators and COD shoppers are physically reachable in any city. Delhivery / Ecom Express / XpressBees public filings + Meesho's own DRHP/annual report carry RTO data.

## Cross-cutting observations

- **Only Valmo ships a data pack.** Every other case requires you to construct your own numbers from public sources.
- **"10x and long-term thinking" is an explicit R2 criterion** in all six. An incrementalist answer caps out.
- **Round 2 requires a working prototype** (from the competition brief, not the case PDFs). Cases differ enormously in how naturally they yield one.
- Meesho is now **publicly listed** — DRHP + quarterly filings are available and almost no student team will mine them. Free "extent of research" credit on any case.
