Where the opportunity is field · n=8 riders

Every other node just moves boxes. The rider is the only person who meets the customer — so that is where the intervention has to land.

The rider’s rule: “CoD = the customer will cancel”

For a prepaid order he makes 2–3 calls to reach the customer. For a CoD order he makes none — he has decided it will fail. The odds it actually cancels are low, but the belief has hardened into a rule.

1Order is CoD & looks risky → “this one’s dead”
↓
2He skips the calls, the wait, the neighbour-ask
↓
3It fails → belief confirmed → the next CoD gets even less

The opening isn’t “make him try harder.” It’s remove the barrier — get a CoD order the same 2–3 calls a prepaid order already gets. Close the effort gap, don’t raise the ceiling.

03 / 30-Day Pilot Meesho DICE · Valmo RTO

Pay the hub for rescued orders — and make sure the rider shares the win.

Not paying for a second trip — paying to break the “CoD is dead” reflex, so a flagged order gets the rider’s normal prepaid playbook: 2–3 calls, a short wait, ask a neighbour.

How the money moves mechanism
Risk score flags the riskiest ~20% of all orders — both payment modes. CoD is weighted higher, but a prepaid buyer with a flagged history can outscore a clean CoD one
↓
Flag rides with the parcel → hub queue + rider app: “high-risk · bonus on delivery”
↓
Meesho pays the hub ₹15 per flagged order delivered — never for “attempted”, never on total volume
↓
Hub splits it with the delivering rider, same day, via the app — attributable, auditable
Hub ~60%Rider ~40%
₹15 per rescued order · ratio tunable · both sides profit from a delivery that would have been an RTO

Field finding (flag as assumption): the hub pays the rider today, not Meesho — so the incentive must route hub→rider. Keeps Valmo asset-light; no new employment relationship.

The 30 days 1 cluster · 3–5 hubs
Day 1–5
Set up. Wire the risk score → hub app. Fix “flagged” = riskiest 20% of orders (~100k/mo). Baseline the prior 8 weeks of RTO for these hubs.
Day 6–19
Run live. ₹15 bonus, split hub/rider, paid daily in-app. Hub dashboard shows bonus per rider.
Day 20–24
Mid-read. Flagged-delivered vs baseline; check the four guardrail metrics for gaming.
Day 25–30
Readout + go / no-go. Rule: ≥ 9 extra deliveries per 100 flagged → self-funding → widen.

If it works → widen to the cluster, then the zone. If it doesn’t → the barrier wasn’t effort, and we’ve learned that for the price of one pilot.

Honest test: the unflagged orders in the same hubs are the control — if their delivery rate is flat while flagged rises, the lift is real, not effort borrowed from elsewhere.

Per 100 flagged orders (baseline ≈60 delivered), bonus ₹15 each. Break-even ≈ 9 extra deliveries. At scale (riskiest 20% of ≈587 mn CoD orders/yr) ≈ ₹80–140 cr/yr net, plus recovered sales.

Measure four things it’s an experiment
Flagged delivered — did the barrier lift?
Unflagged delivered — did effort just move?
Returns after delivery — was anyone pressured?
Net cost / order — does it pay?

Gaming — pays on delivered only → cuts against fake attempts · rider-attributed in-app payout + audit → hub can’t skim · complaint tracking → no coercion · cap flagged at 20%, watch unflagged rate · the score stays Meesho’s, not the hub’s.
